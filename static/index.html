Problem formulation

Problem formulation is the practical first step of a modern risk assessment — it is not one of the four formal Codex steps, but it frames all of them. Define the question, scope, population, endpoint, product/use scenario, data needs, and decision context. KEvidence carries this structured context into hazard identification, hazard characterisation, exposure assessment, and risk characterisation.

Select an output purpose before starting. This workbench organizes evidence for expert review across the four Codex risk-assessment steps; it does not produce risk-management decisions, EFSA conclusions, health-based guidance values, ADIs, ARfDs, or acceptability decisions. AOP structure comes from local AOP-Wiki-derived data; NAM PODs, exposure estimates, IVIVE factors, and thresholds are user-supplied or heuristic unless explicitly connected to curated sources.

Identity & characterisation

Upload the applicant's documents (technical dossier sections, specifications, certificates of analysis, study reports). KEvidence identifies the additive and its components from them, drafts the characterisation in the FEEDAP opinion layout (§1.1.1, Table 1/2, Appendix A) and lists the data gaps against the FEEDAP Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA Journal 2017;15(10):5023). MVP: chemical characterisation, including plant extracts by their constituents; production-strain characterisation is out of scope.

1. Hazard identification

Hazard identification asks: can this agent cause harm? Search candidate Adverse Outcome Pathways by chemical, key event, adverse outcome, or AOP ID, and select the pathway that best represents a plausible route from molecular initiating event to adverse outcome. Identifying a hazard is not yet a risk conclusion — that requires exposure (steps 3–4).

2. Hazard characterisation

Hazard characterisation asks: what is the nature and severity of the harm, and at what dose? Review the selected pathway and its evidence, gather reference points and health-based guidance values (NOAEL/BMD, ADI/TDI/ARfD) from OpenFoodTox, and establish a dose-response point of departure (POD) from NAM data. The POD you set here becomes the hazard side of risk characterisation (step 4).

Dose-response point of departure (POD)

Record the NAM / dose-response point of departure that characterises the hazard. This POD is carried into risk characterisation (4) and compared against the exposure estimate from step 3.

A POD describes the hazard's dose-response; it is not itself a risk. Confirm KE mapping, assay quality, hit call, and units before use.

3. Exposure assessment

Exposure assessment asks: who is exposed, how much, how often, and by which route? For food safety this is usually dietary exposure — concentration in food/feed × consumption pattern × population group × use conditions. Record the scenario and the resulting exposure estimate that will be integrated in risk characterisation (step 4).

The exposure estimate is user-supplied unless connected to a curated dietary-exposure model. Exposure is what turns an intrinsic hazard into a risk — without it, no risk conclusion can be drawn.

4. Risk characterisation

Risk characterisation is the integration step: given the hazard (2) and the exposure (3), what is the risk? KEvidence compares the dose-response POD with the exposure estimate to produce a screening margin (BER / MOE) and a hazard-quotient-style ratio. Hazard is intrinsic potential for harm; risk is that hazard under actual exposure conditions.

This is a prototype screening calculator, not a validated regulatory threshold framework. Screening margins support prioritization and hypothesis refinement unless validated sources and program-specific thresholds are configured.

Scientific opinion / advice

Assemble a structured draft opinion from problem formulation, hazard identification and characterisation, exposure assessment, risk characterisation, and uncertainty analysis. This scientific advice is the output handed to risk managers — risk assessment informs, but does not make, the risk-management decision. Use the assistant to rewrite or explain sections.

Publication — sanitisation under the Transparency Regulation

Before EFSA publishes a document (Art. 38 of the General Food Law as amended by Regulation (EU) 2019/1381), confidential information and personal data are masked. KEvidence proposes what to mask in a PDF according to EFSA's practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality (Decision of the Executive Director, 2021): personal data (Art. 39e GFL; Art. 4(5)), the items listed in the confidentiality request or decision you supply (Art. 3(1)(c), Art. 13), and, as candidates only, passages matching the Annex closed list for the sector. You review every proposal; KEvidence then removes the selected content from the PDF (true redaction, not a black box over the text), scrubs metadata, comments, links and attachments, and verifies the output.

AI configuration

KEvidence's AI features use your own provider, model and key. Nobody else's key is used for you, and yours is used only for you: it is encrypted (AES-256-GCM, bound to your account) and never shown again — only its last four characters. You can save several models and switch between them to compare answers; the one marked In use serves every AI feature. Without one, the AI features are off for you; everything that runs locally (characterisation, gap analysis, sanitisation rules, redaction, AOP search, screening calculations) keeps working.

Quick user manual

A one-page guide to the Codex risk-assessment workflow. Risk assessment is the scientific part of risk analysis (which also covers risk management and risk communication). Problem formulation frames the four Codex steps below; keep every generated statement traceable to source data and expert judgement.

Mandate & question — domain and problem formulation.Choose the domain (every domain gets the workflow and publication; grounded domains also get guidance-checked characterisation), then enter the substance/stressor, use scenario, endpoint of concern and decision context. This frames the four Codex steps.
Identity & characterisation — what exactly is being assessed?In a grounded domain (today: feed additives), upload or link the applicant's documents and click Characterise from documents. KEvidence identifies the additive and its components from the documents (optional details only to correct it), maps the evidence to each requirement of the FEEDAP identity/characterisation guidance (EFSA Journal 2017;15(10):5023), cites document and page for every value, lists data gaps, and exports the characterisation as Markdown.
Hazard identification — can it cause harm?Search AOPs by chemical, key event, adverse outcome, or AOP ID, and select the pathway representing a plausible route from MIE to adverse outcome.
Hazard characterisation — nature, severity, dose.Review KER evidence, pull reference points/HBGVs from OpenFoodTox, and set a dose-response point of departure (POD) mapped to a key event.
Exposure assessment — who, how much, how often, route.Record concentration in food × consumption × population × use conditions, and the resulting exposure estimate.
Risk characterisation — integrate hazard × exposure.Compare the POD with the exposure estimate to obtain a screening margin (BER/MOE). Interpret as prioritisation support, not a final regulatory conclusion.
Uncertainty & scientific opinion.Run the uncertainty & data-gap analysis, then build a draft scientific opinion that separates curated evidence, user-entered data, assumptions, and gaps for handoff to risk managers.
Publication — sanitisation.Before a document is published under the Transparency Regulation, upload the PDF in Publication, choose the sector, enter the applicant (stays public) and paste the items of the confidentiality request or decision. KEvidence proposes personal data and request items (selected) and Annex candidates (not selected), each with its article of EFSA's practical arrangements; you review, then it redacts, scrubs metadata and verifies the output.
AI configuration and activity log.AI features (assistant, narratives, sanitisation AI review) use the provider, model and key you set in AI configuration; without one they are off and everything local still works. Activity log shows what was done under your account.
Your domain is not grounded yet?Every domain gets the workflow. To ground one (e.g. food enzymes, novel foods, pesticides), contribute its guidance PDFs and a catalogue quoting them verbatim, registered in data/guidance/domains.json — no code change. Open an issue on GitHub or email kevidence@devalier.com.
Tip: required fields are the values needed to make the next tab meaningful. If uncertain, record assumptions in the draft caveat.
